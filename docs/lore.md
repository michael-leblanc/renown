# Lore

What people in the world say. None of it is confirmed, and none of it ever is (D68).
The rules behind it are in `docs/decisions.md` (D65 to D120, and D158).

## What everyone agrees on
- Nobody remembers coming. You **wake** already grown, with clothes on your back and hands that know a trade you never learned.
- This land is an island, **Twinring**, named for its two rings of standing stones. It is not the only one. The sea around it is **the Long Water**. Sail in any direction for as long as you like: there is always more sea, and more land beyond it. Those who went looking for the edge came back with nothing to report, or didn't come back.
- The dead stay dead. No one has returned to say where they went.

## What they argue about
- **A human townsman:** "We wake among our own because our god sets us down where we belong. The orcs wake over there for the same reason. That distance was put there on purpose."
- **An orc roamer:** "I woke with a fighter's scars and no memory of earning them. I earned them somewhere, and I died there. This is what comes after. It never ends because the dead never stop coming."
- **A goblin shopkeeper:** "If this is the afterlife, where do we go when we die here? Nobody answers that one."
- **A one-god believer, found in both towns:** "Three peoples, three gods? No. One hand sets us all down. The rest are its shadows."
- **On the war, from either side:** "They eat differently, bury differently, laugh at different things. Nobody remembers who struck first, and nobody needs to."

## The waking place
- Every town grew around one: an old ring of standing stones, older than anything else there. People settled where their kind kept turning up.
- Nobody has ever seen a waking. The ring is empty, you look away, and someone is lying there asleep. People have sat up all night to watch, and each one dozed off, blinked, or turned at a noise.
- You open your eyes on the stones, dressed, with your gear beside you. The first thing a town does is look you over: what you carry tells them what you are, since you can't tell them yourself.
- For a short while, no one can harm someone who has just woken.
- **Believers:** the ring is their god's hand, and the townsfolk keep it swept.
- **Afterlife believers:** it is a door, and it only opens one way.
- **Others:** "The count never changes. One dies, one wakes. Maybe it's the same one, washed clean."
- Dawndoor calls its ring **the Threshold**: you are set down at the door of where you belong. Oldscar's orcs call their ring **the Debt**: you wake owing the scars you carry.
- Oldscar's goblins call the same ring **the Find**. **A goblin:** "Skerra found you and left you there, same as a chest." Orcs think the name is childish.

### Who raised the stones
- **A human:** "Halvar set them. The first threshold, before there was a town."
- **An orc:** "Someone very big. Nobody alive could lift one."
- **A goblin:** "Nobody. They were just found there, like everything else of hers."
- **A one-god believer:** "They are the Hand's fingers."

### Why you wake with no memory
- **A human:** "Halvar keeps it for you behind his door. It is yours, so he keeps it."
- **An orc:** "Whatever you were before proves nothing here. You wake owing, and you start from what people can see."
- **A goblin:** "Skerra kept it. Her coin, for finding you."

### Why some wake with more
- Some wake in fine clothes with a full purse. The towns call them **well-woken**. It earns them nothing: nobody knows their name, and no one owes them a thing.
- **A human:** "Halvar keeps your memory, but what was yours he lets you keep."
- **An orc:** "Fine cloth proves nothing here. Still, a full purse makes anyone look bigger."
- **A goblin:** "Skerra left coins in their pockets. A good find, that one."
- **A one-god believer:** "The Hand gives what it gives."

## The gods
- **Halvar, the Keeper (humans):** god of thresholds, promises and what is yours. Humans say he sets each person down where they belong. His sign is a door found open at dawn.
- **Gorrath, the Scarred (orcs):** you are what you have done, not what you own. Orcs wake with scars they can't explain and say he put them there as a debt to live up to. His sign is an old scar that aches before a fight.
- **Skerra, the Finder (goblins):** goddess of luck and quick wits. Goblins say she hides things in the world for the clever to find, which is why new chests keep appearing. Her sign is something found in a place you had already searched.
- **The One Hand (a creed, in both towns):** "One hand sets us all down." Its believers say there is only one god and the others are false, or just faces of it. Neither town fully trusts them. It has no sign of its own: its believers say the open door, the aching scar and the lucky find are all the same hand at work.

### What the others answer the One Hand
- **A human:** "Believe what you like, as long as you keep your word."
- **An orc:** "A hand with no scars has done nothing."
- **A goblin:** "Skerra has already been through that Hand's pockets."
- Nobody fights over it.

### What the gods look like
- No god has ever shown up. These are only pictures in people's heads.
- **A human:** "Nobody has seen Halvar. We have seen his door. We don't guess."
- **An orc:** "Gorrath is the biggest orc there ever was. More scars than skin."
- **A goblin:** "Skerra is smaller than any of us. That's why nobody ever catches her leaving things."
- **A one-god believer:** "There is only a hand."

## Humans, orcs and goblins
- Humans measure a person by what they keep and promise. Orcs measure a person by strength, and deeds and scars are the proof of it. That is most of why they can't agree.
- Orcs go by what they see. Someone big is strong. A big weapon is strong, a small weapon is weak. A big stranger is respected before doing anything; a small one has to prove it.
- **An orc, on goblins:** "Gorrath made us to stand and fight. Their goddess made them to pick up after."
- **A goblin, on orcs:** "They think little of us. They also eat because we trade."
- Orcs think little of goblins because goblins are small.
- **A goblin saying:** "Talk to a dolt with something big in your hand." Some goblins carry a weapon too big to use, just for show. Orcs know the trick and still half believe it.
- Goblins are the smarter of the two, and they call orcs **dolts**. Orcs think the word means "the big ones", and some say it with pride: "the biggest dolt in Oldscar". No goblin has corrected them.

### What they say of the enemy
- **An orc, on humans:** "You can't read a Hoarder. They come in every size, and a small one can hit like a big one."
- **A human, on orcs:** "A Scarred keeps its word to someone bigger and forgets it with someone smaller. That is no promise at all."
- **A goblin, on humans:** "Hoarders lock everything up. That only tells you where the good things are."

## The two towns
- **Dawndoor** is the human town, on the east shore. Its people call themselves **the Keeping**: you are what you keep and what you promise. They say the town is named for Halvar's sign, a door found open at dawn.
- An old stone doorway stands alone on the east side of Dawndoor, with no wall around it, facing the Long Water and the sunrise. Humans say it is Halvar's door and that the town grew beside it. Nobody walks through it at night, only at dawn.
- **In Oldscar, on that door:** "It's a ruin. What's left of a house."
- **Oldscar** is the town of the orcs and goblins, inland in the west, away from the water. Its people call themselves **the Tally**, and they say the town is named for Gorrath's sign, the old scar that aches before a fight.
- Oldscar stands under a cliff with one long old crack down it. Orcs say it is Gorrath's scar, and that the town was put there because of it.
- **A goblin, on the cliff, when no orc is near:** "It's a crack."
- **An orc, on the name:** "The Tally is what each of us has done. Nothing else is counted."
- **A goblin, on the name:** "The Tally is what was bought and what was sold. Ask an orc who keeps it."
- In Dawndoor, everyone from Oldscar is **"the Scarred"**, goblins included. Goblins dislike it: the scars are the orcs', and so is the god.
- In Oldscar, the humans are **"the Hoarders"**: people who count a person by what they keep.

## The dead
- **Humans** bury their dead under a doorstone, with one thing that was theirs.
- **Orcs** burn theirs and tell the deeds aloud.
- **Goblins** trade away what the dead owned and keep one coin for Skerra.
- **One-god believers** get their own people's custom, and other believers leave the dead's right hand open, palm up. "The Hand takes back what it gave."
- No custom says where the dead go.

### A dead enemy
- **A human:** "Leave it where it fell. Not ours to keep."
- **An orc:** "One who fought well gets its deeds told, same as ours. A coward gets nothing."
- **A goblin, taking what is on it:** "Skerra left it."

### A dead outcast
- **Humans** bury them outside the town, with no doorstone and nothing of theirs. "Kept no promise, so nothing is kept for them."
- **Orcs** burn them like anyone and tell every deed, the bad ones too. "The Tally leaves nothing out."
- **Goblins** trade their goods as usual but keep no coin for Skerra. "A bad find."

## The Long Water
- Humans are the ones who talk of sailing.
- **An orc:** "Nothing across the water is worth a scar."
- **A goblin:** "Skerra's best finds wash up on the shore."

### What lies across it
- Nobody on Twinring has reached another shore and come back to tell it. Everyone guesses.
- **A human:** "Other rings, other peoples. Every shore has a threshold."
- **An orc:** "Anyone big over there would have come and taken Twinring by now. So they're small."
- **A goblin:** "That's where Skerra keeps what she hasn't left yet."

## Further out
- Humans, orcs and goblins are not the only peoples. Skeletons, demons and zombies wake too, around rings of their own, somewhere further out.
- They are peoples like any other. None of them is good or evil for what it is.
- Beasts are animals. They don't wake at any ring, and nobody counts them among the peoples.
