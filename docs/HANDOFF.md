# Handoff — Renown

Updated: 2026-11-14

## Goal
- A small, self-hosted multiplayer isometric roleplay sandbox: friends share one world, but each player lives their own life and grows as strong as they want, on their own path.
- Why: in co-op games like Divinity or Baldur's Gate 3, friends are kept so close together that when one takes the loot the others get nothing and have no other choice. Here each player can go their own way.
- Mostly for Michael and his friends. Not planned for sale.

## Vision (Michael's requirements)
- **Multiplayer, not an MMO:** players host their own server, about 10 players (could be fewer or more).
- **Isometric.**
- **Roleplay.**
- **Own life:** a player can play without interacting with the others, but can if they want to, or when they run into them. They can go explore and loot a different area, a bit like Enshrouded.
- **PvP and PvE.**
- **Quests, NPCs, a living world.**
- **Quests come out of the world, not from a list we write (Michael, 2026-11-14):** "My goal is that all this generates quests without us predefining quests." His examples: if someone is very bad for a faction, officials or guilds might ask to have that person killed; if a person hates another personally, they might ask to have them killed; if a person owns a store and needs a certain resource, they might ask you to fetch some.
- **Living NPCs, and a lot of them:** NPCs live their own lives. One sees loot a player left, takes it and gets stronger. One who kills a player rises in rank (in the spirit of Shadow of Mordor, not a copy). Some keep a shop, some hunt, some take quests and kill monsters. If performance is a problem, not all of them have to be full AIs.
- **Factions and races:** several of each. A race is not good or evil in itself (think WoW: orcs aren't really evil). Some sub-factions can be evil, and that is true for humans too.
- **Random birth:** on joining a server the player chooses nothing. Race, name, class and everything else are random. One player joins as a noble human with "high" intellect (high for a new player); another as some kind of monster with low intellect and high strength.
- **World shaped by each player's actions:**
  - Murdering NPCs or players makes the towns nearest the murder see that player as evil. The fame can spread to the whole world.
  - Good deeds are recognised and spread the same way.
  - Helping one faction more can win it territory, or simply make it stronger than the other factions.
- **A world that is alive and changes:** new chests appear. If possible (maybe not in the first version), new places appear too: bandit camps, goblin camps and so on.
- **Perception:** people build a memory of you, personal and shared. Traits like "dread", "evil", "good" say how you are perceived, and each faction also has its own opinion of you.
- **Ultimate goal:** a player can aim to become a kind of boss that others try to kill, for being too powerful and too evil, or too good.
- **Overpowered, but hard to reach (Michael, 2026-11-14):** "I want players to feel like they can become overpowered in some way that players need to team to take them down. But it's hard to get there. I would say eventually a player could start to enchant its items, gain powerful rare spells, etc."
- **Being the boss is one path, not the goal (Michael, 2026-11-14):** "the goal of each player is not necessarily to be the 'boss'. You do whatever you want, but some people might want to be evil."
- **A generated world (Michael, 2026-11-14):** "My vision is that the world is generated, including the quests, environments, npcs/players (waking), economy, etc." For environments he chose a land generated from a seed (D136).
- **Death:** permadeath (D3).
- **References:** V Rising is the closest he knows, but not really the same. Enshrouded for the freedom to roam apart. Shadow of Mordor for NPCs that grow. RuneScape and Ultima Online for click-to-move control (D16). No MMOs.

## Decided
- Name: Renown — after the reputation that spreads from town to town.
- Playbook full mode, one repo.
- Separate project from Crown of the Tomb — different game, different art.
- Personal use — licence terms are not a blocker; keep the art in a private repo.
- **Stack (D1):** Godot 4.7 pinned, GDScript only. One project: desktop clients plus a headless Linux server. Server-authoritative over ENet. GUT tests from `scripts/verify.sh`.
- **Free to split up (D2):** nothing keeps players together. Loot is shared world loot, never per-player.
- **Permadeath (D3):** death is final, then a fresh random birth. The corpse's gear stays as world loot.
- **One character model (D4):** an NPC is a player character driven by AI. No "players only" or "NPCs only" rule without a new decision.
- **Witnesses (D5):** a deed counts only if a character sees it or the body is found. The witness must reach a town; word then spreads town to town and fades.
- **Three levels of detail (D6):** full near a player, coarse in the region, abstract far away. Rule-based AI. The NPC budget comes from a measurement.
- **Reputation (D7 to D9):** traits say how a character is perceived: Morality, Mercy, Fame (only grows). Each faction keeps its own standing. Standing sets guards and prices; traits set fear and bounties. A fourth trait is a new decision.
- **Races (D10):** Human, Orc and Goblin are playable first. A race is never good or evil; factions and sub-factions are.
- **First slice (D11):** a headless server and two clients; one hand-built region, two towns, two factions; random birth; move, fight, loot, die, reborn; townsfolk, guards and roamers at full detail; a witnessed deed travels town to town; guards and prices react. After the slice: fear, bounties, quests, NPC jobs, the coarse and abstract levels, more regions.
- **Slice factions (D12, "for now"):** a human faction and an orc-and-goblin faction, one town each. Rivals, not good against evil. Born into your race's faction; harming your own people can lose you your own town. Named in D77.
- **At war (D14, "for now"; replaces D13):** the other town's guards attack on sight, and NPCs of one side attack characters of the other on sight in the wilderness, player or NPC. Deeds cannot win the enemy faction over. Michael expects to soften this later.
- **Towns replace their dead (D15):** a new random-birth NPC appears in its faction's town after a delay, never above the starting population.
- **Combat (D16 to D18):** click to move and click to attack, like RuneScape and Ultima Online. Attacks repeat on their own at the weapon's speed; each hit, block and dodge is a roll on stats. Characters stand on a tile grid and walk in 8 directions.
- **Character (D19 to D21, D24):** four stats: Strength, Agility, Endurance, Intellect (learns faster). Skills grow by use. No level: a stranger is judged by gear and reputation. A class is a background (noble, hunter, thief) that sets starting skills and gear and locks nothing.
- **Six talents (D25, "for now"):** born with; a talent opens skills others can never learn. Gifted (magic), Mighty, Voice, Keen senses, Beast-kin, Silver tongue. Most characters have none or one. Stealth is a normal skill.
- **Knowledge (D22, D23):** the slice tracks standing, Morality and Fame; Mercy waits. Inspecting a character shows only what your own character knows. You learn by seeing a deed, by asking an NPC what its town knows, or (if possible) by overhearing NPCs talk.
- **Zero health (D26):** in the slice the character dies at once. The fallen state (winner finishes or spares) is the first thing after the slice and brings in Mercy.
- **Saved world (D27):** the slice saves the whole world to disk now and then and on shutdown, and loads it on start. Every feature that adds state also saves and loads it. The format stays with the server design.
- **NPC judgement (D28):** an NPC judges an enemy as a player does, from gear and from what it has heard or remembers, never from true stats. Guards and roamers fight unless the enemy looks clearly stronger; townsfolk run to the guards. An NPC can be wrong.
- **Strangers (D29, D30):** a character's name is hidden until your character learns it. Race shows unless an item hides it (clothes covering the body or face). A deed is attached to a face, not a name: you recognise someone you heard about or saw act when their face is showing; a covered face is not recognised.
- **Names are told (D31):** a name is learned only by being told, never by standing near someone. The first conversation with an NPC you have never met can open with "introduce yourself". A player shares their name with another player by hand (right-click on them). An NPC can give you someone's name when it tells you about them. An NPC may ask your name during a conversation; you can refuse, and it reacts to that.
- **Crimes (D32):** in the slice a crime is a deed against your own faction: attacking or killing one of your own people, or taking what belongs to someone (shop stock, goods in a home). Hitting back at someone who struck first is not a crime. It counts only with a witness or a found body (D5). Lesser offences (trespass, looting your own dead) are not in the slice.
- **Covered face (D33):** a deed done with the face covered still spreads from town to town, but it attaches to no one: the doer's standing and traits don't move. If a witness saw the face at any point, the deed counts as normal.
- **Region size (D34, "for now"):** the slice's region is small, about 2 minutes' walk between the two towns, with wilderness between and around them. Just to test; Michael means to expand it.
- **Places own things (D35):** everything inside a shop or a home belongs to whoever works or lives there. Anything outdoors on the ground, or on a corpse, is free world loot (D2). Taking from inside a building that isn't yours is theft (D32). No owner tag on items.
- **Property (D36):** no building, but a player can buy a ready-made house or shop in a town and put items up for sale; players and NPCs buy. A bit like The Guild. When the owner dies it goes back on sale, and what was inside comes with it, in a chest. Built after the slice, alongside NPC jobs (same NPC economy).
- **Item value (D37):** every item has a value. The price of a house or shop on sale goes up with the value of what is in its chest. Value is a reference, a bit like RuneScape: it is not what a player has to pay, and players can pay more between themselves.
- **Refusing your name (D38):** an NPC that doesn't have your name treats you as a stranger and holds back, and how far depends on its role: some won't talk until you introduce yourself, a guard might not let you pass, a shopkeeper cares less. It also depends on how you are perceived: people who dread you are too scared to oppose you. The NPC goes by what it knows of you (D28), never true stats.
- **Dread (D39):** not a fourth trait. Dread is the low end of Mercy, as each NPC knows it: an NPC dreads a character it knows never spares anyone. A good character can be dreaded. It arrives after the slice, with Mercy and the fallen state (D26).
- **Covered face in town (D40):** only guards care about a covered face; townsfolk and shopkeepers don't. A guard always challenges one, crime or no crime, unless it is scared of the character (D39). A face counts as covered only when the item worn has the property "covers face"; an ordinary helmet does not.
- **Refusing to uncover (D41):** the guard orders the character out of town, and attacks if they stay or push past. No standing is lost (D33). The player can uncover, leave or fight.
- **Nameless in the slice (D42):** townsfolk and guards won't say what the town knows until you introduce yourself. Shopkeepers serve anyway, and nobody blocks the way. The harder reactions in D38 come after the slice.
- **Stranger at home (D43):** nobody has a newborn's name, not even their own town, and the newborn has nobody's. Same for replacement NPCs (D15).
- **Names spread (D44):** a name given to one NPC joins what its town knows, like a deed. One introduction opens the whole town, the name then travels with any tale about the character, and it can't be taken back.
- **Unseen theft (D45):** no witness, no crime (D5). Goods taken with nobody watching are just gone; in front of the owner it is still a crime. "The owner notices the loss" is a candidate for later.
- **Dead owner (D46):** a dead NPC's shop or home belongs to nobody: what is inside is free world loot until another NPC takes the place over (D15). The killing is judged on its own (D32, D14). Player-bought property follows D36.
- **Replacement roles (D47):** a dead guard or shopkeeper is replaced by one of the town's townsfolk, who takes the role and the shop. The newcomer (D15) is always a townsperson and takes the spot that opened. The town assigns the role; NPCs picking jobs still comes after the slice. This changes D11's "fixed roles".
- **Who is chosen (D48):** the townsperson with the best fighting skills becomes the guard; a random one becomes the shopkeeper (no trade skill yet).
- **Offline (D49):** a character stays in the world for 30 seconds after its player logs off, then disappears and can't be touched. It comes back at the same spot.
- **Knowledge dies (D50):** a new life knows nothing and nobody from the life before. What a town knows stays with the town and can be asked for again.
- **Overhearing (D51):** not in the slice; right after it. In the slice a character learns by seeing a deed or by asking an NPC.
- **Tiles (D52):** one character per tile at rest. A character can walk through others but must end on a free tile. No body-blocking.
- **War in the slice (D53):** NPCs fight the enemy only when they meet. Guards stay in town, roamers wander the wilderness. No patrols or raids until the region grows.
- **Living world (D54):** loot comes back as new chests that appear in the world, not as the same chest refilling. If possible, new places appear over time (bandit camps, goblin camps); not necessarily in the first version.
- **Slice spawns (D55):** the slice has new chests only, appearing in the wilderness out of sight of any player. Camps come after the slice but are planned for: build a general "something appears in the world" system, and keep factions as data. A camp can belong to a sub-faction or to a faction itself.
- **Backing off (D56):** a roamer that judges an enemy clearly stronger runs to its town. A guard falls back to the other guards and fights with them; it never leaves the town. NPCs don't count heads yet.
- **Dead roamer (D57):** replaced by a townsperson, the best fighter left, like a guard (D47, D48). Guard posts are filled before roamer spots.
- **Dying on purpose (D58, "for now"):** nothing stops it. No reroll button and no wait before a rebirth. A wait before every rebirth is a candidate for later.
- **Cast out (D59):** a character their own town attacks on sight can earn their way back: standing with their own faction recovers. Until then they are an outlaw, attacked by both sides. The enemy town stays closed.
- **The way back (D60):** an outcast's standing recovers only through good deeds that are seen: killing an enemy or defending one of your own, in front of your own people. Not with time. A covered face doesn't count (D33).
- **Empty chest (D61):** it stays for a while, then disappears out of sight of any player.
- **New chest (D62):** it appears on a random free wilderness tile, out of sight of any player and off the roads, up to a maximum number of chests in the region.
- **Gutted town (D63):** with an empty post and no townsperson left, the next newcomer takes the post directly: guards first, then roamers, then the shopkeeper.
- **Slice population (D64, "for now"):** 16 NPCs per town, 32 in the region: 8 townsfolk, 4 guards, 3 roamers, 1 shopkeeper. Kept in data; the real budget comes from the measurement (D6).
- **Endless world (D65):** the world never ends and nobody has found an edge. It is called "the world" or "the land", never a planet. Nobody knows how old it is; there may be no end to its races.
- **Everyone arrives (D66):** every character arrives as an adult with no memory of anything before. Nobody is born here; no children. A random birth (D3) and a town's newcomer (D15) are arrivals. Nobody knows what happens to those who die here.
- **Gods and creeds (D67):** there are gods, and some believe in the "one true god". Some gods are closer to some races, so people think that god brought that race. Some believe this world is the afterlife (an orc died in its own world and came here), which would be why it is endless. Gods never show up in person (for now); people only see signs.
- **Lore is belief (D68):** the game never confirms a theory. Lore comes from what NPCs say, and towns disagree.
- **Why they fight (D69):** the humans and the orcs-and-goblins are at war (D14) because the two peoples are so different that they just don't agree. Not about creed, not about land. Neither side is good or evil (D10).
- **Waking (D70):** arriving is called "waking". You wake with gear and skills but no memory (this is what a background is, D24), near your own kind. Lore is written in plain townsfolk voices; the first draft is in `docs/lore.md`.
- **Waking place (D71):** each town grew around an old ring of standing stones where its people wake. Nobody has ever seen a waking: a newcomer (D15) or a reborn player appears there only while no player has it in sight, like a new chest (D62). Michael answered the camping risk, not the place itself; taken as a yes.
- **Waking protection (D72, D73, "for now"):** a newly woken player can't be harmed for a few minutes. It ends early the moment the character attacks, steals or leaves their town. It is the answer to an enemy waiting at the waking place. A first version; Michael doesn't want it over-designed.
- **Three gods and a creed (D74, "for now"):** one god close to each race (humans, orcs, goblins). The "one true god" is a creed, not a fourth god: believers of any race, in both towns, say there is only one.
- **Orcs and goblins (D75):** they work together, but orcs think little of goblins.
- **The gods (D76):** humans, Halvar the Keeper (thresholds, promises, what is yours); orcs, Gorrath the Scarred (you are what you have done); goblins, Skerra the Finder (luck and wits; goblins say she leaves the chests that appear, D54); the creed, the One Hand. Humans measure a person by what they keep and promise, orcs by strength, which deeds and scars prove (D114): that is the "so different" of D69. All of it is in `docs/lore.md`.
- **Names (D77):** the human faction is **the Keeping**, town **Dawndoor**. The orc-and-goblin faction is **the Tally**, town **Oldscar**. No race in a name. The Keeping: humans measure a person by what they keep and promise. The Tally: orcs hear a tally of deeds, goblins a tally of trade (D75). Each town is named for its god's sign: Halvar's door found open at dawn, Gorrath's old scar. Chosen by Michael on 2026-11-13.
- **Nicknames (D78):** Dawndoor calls everyone in Oldscar "the Scarred", goblins included, and goblins dislike it. Oldscar calls the humans "the Hoarders". Nicknames only; the factions are still the Keeping and the Tally.
- **One Hand in the slice (D79):** a believer is just a townsperson of its own town's faction, with no standing of its own. The creed is only something they say. A sub-faction is a candidate for later.
- **Other peoples (D80):** skeletons, demons and zombies are peoples like the others, not monsters. They wake around rings of their own, further out, and none is good or evil in itself (D10). They are characters (D4). Not in the slice.
- **Modern zombies (D81):** the modern zombie art (cop, fireman, soldier, worker, radioactive) is not used. Only the fantasy-looking zombies are.
- **Oldscar roles (D82):** in the starting population, guards and roamers are orcs, the shopkeeper is an orc or a goblin, and townsfolk are a mix. Replacement rules are unchanged (D47, D48), so a goblin can become a guard later.
- **Signs in the slice (D83):** a god's sign is only a story NPCs tell. A player never sees one, and nothing is built for it. Signs you can see are a candidate for later.
- **Regions (D84, "hand-built" replaced by D136):** the world is hand-built, region by region, and new regions (islands and other lands) are unlocked over time, "a bit WoW style". Nothing is generated without end. The lore still calls the land endless (D65).
- **First island (D85):** the first region, with Dawndoor and Oldscar, is an island. The sea is the end of the map, and later regions are across the water. The lore says "sail in any direction" and nobody has found an edge.
- **Twinring (D86):** the first island is called Twinring, for its two rings of standing stones, one in each town.
- **Skerra's sign (D87):** something found in a place you had already searched. Only a story in the slice, like the other signs (D83).
- **Protection for NPCs (D88):** an NPC newcomer gets the same waking protection as a player (D72, D73), and loses it the same way. Not a players-only rule (D4).
- **Protection shows (D89):** a protected character has a soft glow or a small marker that everyone sees, gone the moment the protection ends.
- **Protection length (D90):** 3 minutes to start, stored as a setting. It still ends early on an attack, a theft or leaving town (D73).
- **Hearing lore (D91):** in the slice a conversation has one more choice, "What do people say here?". The NPC answers with one line from `docs/lore.md` that fits its town and people. Lines are data, tagged with who can say them.
- **Lore for strangers (D92):** an NPC shares lore with anyone, name or no name. Town knowledge about people and deeds still needs an introduction (D42).
- **Protection scope (D93):** waking protection stops only harm from other players and from AI. It is not general invulnerability. Michael: "it protects only against other players/AI".
- **NPCs and the glow (D94):** an enemy NPC ignores a character under waking protection: no attack, no chase, no waiting. Once the glow ends it treats them normally. Michael: "it just ignores it" (read as: ignores the character; he was told this reading).
- **Dolts (D95):** goblins are smarter than orcs (Michael's lore), and goblins call orcs "dolts". Orcs don't really understand the word.
- **Stat leans (D96):** at a random birth goblins tend to roll higher Intellect and orcs higher Strength. The ranges overlap, so a clever orc or a strong goblin is possible. Same for players and NPC newcomers.
- **Humans (D97):** humans roll every stat evenly, with no lean. Goblins lean clever, orcs lean strong, humans are the wide mix.
- **Beasts (D98):** the beasts in the art packs are animals, not peoples and not characters (D4): no waking, no towns, no faction, and a much simpler AI. Waking protection covers them (D93).
- **The Long Water (D99):** the sea around Twinring. Both towns use the name.
- **Ring names (D100):** Dawndoor's ring is "the Threshold" (Halvar, god of thresholds). Oldscar's ring is "the Debt" (scars are a debt to live up to).
- **One Hand's sign (D101):** none of its own. Its believers say all three gods' signs are the One Hand's.
- **Shore (D102):** in the slice the shore is the limit: no swimming, no sailing, water tiles can't be walked on. Sailing can come later as the way to a new island.
- **No animals in the slice (D103):** the slice keeps D11's scope. Animals arrive right after it.
- **Customs for the dead (D104):** humans bury theirs under a doorstone with one thing that was theirs; orcs burn theirs and tell the deeds aloud; goblins trade away what the dead owned and keep one coin for Skerra. Only things NPCs say in the slice (D91): nothing is built, and a corpse's gear stays world loot (D3). No custom says where the dead go (D66, D68).
- **Answers to the One Hand (D105):** to the claim that all signs are the One Hand's (D101), humans shrug ("believe what you like, as long as you keep your word"), orcs say a hand with no scars has done nothing, and goblins joke that Skerra has already been through the One Hand's pockets. Nobody fights over it (D69). Only things NPCs say (D91).
- **What "dolts" means (D106):** orcs think "dolts" (D95) means "the big ones", a word of respect. Some use it with pride ("the biggest dolt in Oldscar"). No goblin corrects them; it is the goblins' private joke. Only something NPCs say (D91).
- **Oldscar's scar (D107):** Oldscar stands under a cliff with one long old crack down it. Orcs say it is Gorrath's scar and that the town was put there because of it; goblins say it is just a crack, but not in front of orcs. Scenery on the hand-built map: it does nothing, and the game never says who is right (D68).
- **Dawndoor's door (D108):** an old stone doorway stands alone on the east side of Dawndoor, with no wall around it. Humans say it is Halvar's door and that the town grew beside it; by custom nobody walks through it at night, only at dawn. Oldscar says it is a ruin, what is left of a house. Scenery on the hand-built map: it does nothing, the custom is only said (no rule stops a player), and the game never says who is right (D68).
- **The Find (D109):** goblins call Oldscar's ring "the Find" (orcs say "the Debt", D100): each newcomer is something Skerra found and left there, like a chest (D76). Orcs think the goblin name is childish. In play only the lore lines differ: a goblin NPC says "the Find", an orc NPC "the Debt" (D91).
- **One Hand's dead (D110):** a believer gets their own people's custom (D104), and other believers add one gesture: the dead's right hand is left open, palm up ("the Hand takes back what it gave"). Non-believers let them (D105). Only said in the slice (D91).
- **Enemy dead (D111):** humans leave an enemy's body where it fell ("not ours to keep"); orcs tell the deeds of an enemy who fought well, like one of their own, and a coward gets nothing; goblins take what is on it and say Skerra left it. Only things NPCs say (D91): nothing is built, and the gear is world loot either way (D3).

The one difference from UO: defence is the Dodge skill (D151), where UO uses the defender's weapon skill. Michael asked how UO and RuneScape do it, then agreed on 2026-11-14 ("We can").
- **What the player times, and what they don't (D162):** dodging and blocking are never timed by the player: no moving out of the way, no clicking to dodge or block in time. They are rolls (D161). The player can time other things: healing themselves, triggering a skill, casting a spell. Combat stays as simple as RuneScape or UO. Michael, 2026-11-14: "I don't want to react by moving, clicking to dodge or block on time ... You can heal yourself on time, trigger a skill on time / a spell on time". This sharpens D16 to D18.
- **Healing (D164):** in the slice, health comes back slowly on its own, only out of a fight, and there is a bandage item: the player uses it at the moment they choose (D162), it takes a few seconds, and shops and chests hold it (D141). After the slice a Healing skill is added, as in UO; it counts against the total cap (D140). Rejected for the slice: regeneration only, and the Healing skill now. Chosen by Michael on 2026-11-14: "B and C after slice". The numbers are not chosen.
- **Combat must work with the art already owned (D163):** Michael, 2026-11-14: "make sure it works with the assets, I don't want to spend too much on assets". Checked against the art check: D161 needs no new art. A hit is an attack animation plus `TakeDamage`; a dodge can use `Slide` or `Rolling`, or just a "miss" over the head; the Creator has 7 shields; a block has no animation, so it is shown with a reused animation or a flash and a sound (open, see Next steps). Not yet checked by eye: whether one of the Creator's attack animations is a bow shot, and what an arrow in flight is drawn with (nothing in the Creator flies to a target; a plain drawn line or a tileset effect would do).
- **Fight length (D165, "for now"):** a fight between two equal newborns lasts about 30 seconds, which is 7 to 8 hits to kill. Starting numbers, all settings, to tune: newborn health about 40, a bronze sword about 5 damage after armour, one swing every 2 seconds, half of them hitting between equals (D161). Why: with permadeath (D3) a life should end because of a choice, not three bad rolls; the bandage (D164) and running away (D52) only matter if the fight outlasts them; it is about what melee takes in UO and RuneScape, where timed healing stretches it further (D162); and five on one (D135) lasts long enough to be a battle. Rejected: about 10 seconds (2 to 3 hits: luck decides and a bandage is useless) and about a minute (slow to watch, and nearly everyone escapes). Michael asked how UO and RuneScape do it and what Claude recommends, then agreed on 2026-11-14 ("Yes"). Candidate for later, from UO: fast weak weapons against slow heavy ones; it needs no new art (D163).
- **Starting skills (D166, "for now"):** a newborn wakes with 30 in each of its background's skills and 0 in every other skill (skills run 0 to 100, D161). So the background matters from the first minute (a Soldier with a sword hits 15% more often than a Woodcutter who picks one up), the road to 100 is long (D134), and a veteran at 80 hits a newborn about 9 times in 10 and is hit about 1 time in 4 (D135). Nothing is locked: the 0s grow by use (D24). Known cost, told to Michael: a new life starts weak, so dying stings more; waking protection covers the first minutes (D72). Only Hunter and Brawler wake with Dodge (D152), so most newborns are hit more than half the time; health is tuned so a fight still lasts about 30 seconds (D165). Same for NPC newcomers (D4). The number is a setting on the background's row of data. Rejected: 50 in the background's skills (how UO starts a character; half the road is done at birth) and every skill low and about equal, 10 to 15 (a background would be mostly gear). Suggested by Claude, chosen by Michael on 2026-11-14 ("A").
- **No timed skills in the slice (D167):** the bandage (D164) is the only thing a player triggers at a chosen moment in the slice. Combat is auto-attack (D17) plus one decision: fight on, bandage or run. "Use it now, then wait before using it again" is built as a general system with the bandage as its first user, so abilities are added later as rows of data, and NPCs use the same system (D4). Why: the slice is there to prove the living world (witnesses, reputation, towns reacting), and that needs no abilities. Rejected for the slice: one ability everyone has (a kick that interrupts a bandage, or a sprint), and one ability per weapon skill. Both stay candidates for after the slice; the Creator has `Attack1` to `Attack6`, `Special1` and `Kick` to draw them with (D163). Suggested by Claude, chosen by Michael on 2026-11-14 ("A").

- **A hit interrupts a bandage (D168):** bandaging is stopped by any hit taken, so a character has to get away from the fight first and bandage afterwards. A fight is "win or run", and the bandage is for between fights or after an escape. Rejected: hits don't interrupt it but the character stops attacking while bandaging (Claude's suggestion), and UO's way (it finishes, but each hit taken makes it heal less). Chosen by Michael on 2026-11-14 ("A"). Consequence: whether a character who runs can still be hit decides how often a bandage gets used at all (open, see Next steps).

- **Bandage numbers (D169, "for now"):** a bandage takes 4 seconds standing still, and moving cancels it. It heals about 15 of a newborn's 40 health (D165). A bandage that is interrupted (D168) or cancelled is not used up: the character keeps it and loses only the time. Health still comes back slowly on its own out of a fight (D164); the bandage is the fast way. All settings. Proposed by Claude, agreed by Michael on 2026-11-14 ("Yes").

- **A chaser can hit a runner (D170):** an attacker next to a character who is running away keeps swinging as normal, at the same speed and with the same chance to hit (D161); close to RuneScape and later UO. Everyone moves at the same speed and nobody blocks the way (D52), so a chaser stays right behind. Running from an equal or stronger enemy therefore doesn't save a character unless help is near (their own guards, D56), and the bandage is mostly for between fights (D168). The decision that matters is made before the fight: judging a stranger from gear and reputation (D21, D131) and choosing whether to get close. A group can run down a character at the top (D135). Same rule for NPCs (D4), so a roamer that backs off (D56) can be cut down on its way home. Rejected: a swing needs the attacker to stand still (running would always work, and a top character could walk away from five attackers), and a moving character hits half as often (Claude's suggestion). Chosen by Michael on 2026-11-14 ("B"). Not checked: whether the Creator has an attack-while-running animation; if not, the normal attack is reused on the move (D163).

- **Four weapon classes (D171, "for now"):** in the slice a weapon's speed and damage come from its class, and the material moves the damage (D133). Unarmed: fast and weak, about 1.5 seconds and 2 damage. One-handed: the baseline, can hold a shield, 2 seconds and 5. Two-handed: slow and heavy, no shield, 3 seconds and 8. Bows: at range, 2.5 seconds and 4. Speed and damage are fields on every weapon's row of data, so each weapon kind can get its own numbers later, as in UO. Michael, 2026-11-14: "Start with B, maybe later C". Armour takes a fixed amount off each hit (D161), so heavy hits are better against armour and fast weak ones worse; a big weapon also impresses orcs (D114) and needs Strength (D139). The skill used still follows the weapon's kind (D151). Rejected for now: every weapon the same, and every kind with its own numbers. Which Creator melee parts are two-handed waits for the parts to be named.
